From reading the configuration
  AWSNFW_FRAGMENTS: UDP fragments that no stateless rule matches are handled by aws:drop, other packets by the default for full packets; the model has no fragments, and the converted policy follows the default for full packets. Fragments of other protocols are dropped
  AWSNFW_ASYMMETRIC: one direction of some connections is passed by the stateless rules and the other forwarded to the stateful engine, which then sees half a flow and may drop it (sl-edge-8, the stateless default action aws:forward_to_sfe). The converted policy leaves those connections to the stateful rules
  AWSNFW_STATELESS_FORWARD: these stateless rules forward what they match to the stateful engine, so the stateless rules below them do not see it: sl-edge-10
  AWSNFW_STATELESS_NOT_WORKED_OUT: some stateless rules cannot be read (TCP flags, a rule group not in the input, a value that cannot be read): they are not converted, and what the rules after them and the replies meet was worked out as if they matched nothing
  AWSNFW_ALERT_RULES: 1 alert rule decides nothing (an alert only logs) and was left out
  AWSNFW_CUSTOM_ACTIONS: custom actions publish metrics to CloudWatch; they decide nothing and are not converted
  AWSNFW_GROUP_MISSING: the policy names rule groups that are not in the input: ThreatSignaturesMalwareStrictOrder. Their rules are not converted; add the answers of describe-rule-group
  AWSNFW_HOME_NET: HOME_NET is 10.20.0.0/16, 2001:db8:20::/56 (the CIDR blocks of VPC vpc-0a1b2c3d4e5f60789); EXTERNAL_NET is everything else, unless a rule group sets it
  AWSNFW_NO_ZONES: the firewall sits in a route path and names no zones or interfaces: the converted policy has none. A target with zones needs the networks behind them (--zone-network)

Notes
  The policy names no zones, so the matrix has one cell. Give zones as networks with --network-zone NAME=NETWORK[,NETWORK...] to see who reaches whom.
  4 rule(s) never match and are left out; 'fwshift audit' names them.
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  app:NAME: allowed for that application only. Which ports it uses is not looked up here.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
