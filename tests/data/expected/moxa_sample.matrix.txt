From reading the configuration
  MOXA_SOURCE: EDR-810-VPN-2GSFP: 10 policies of the Layer 3 policy, first match by index; zones are the interfaces lan, wan1 and wan2. IPv4 only
  MOXA_DEFAULT_ACCEPT: what no policy matches is read as passing: the configuration names no default action, and FWShift found none for the classic firmware in Moxa's documentation (not confirmed on a device). If the router drops it, add a policy at the end of the converted policy that drops everything
  MOXA_NAT_ORDER: port forwarding and 1-to-1 NAT are read as done before the Layer 3 policy, whose policies name the inside address (as iptables does it on Linux; Moxa's documentation does not say, not confirmed): the policies were rewritten for the address as it arrives. N-to-1 NAT is done after it
  MOXA_TRUSTED_ACCESS: trusted access, which limits who reaches the router itself; not converted: lan 192.168.127.0 255.255.255.0 enable
  MOXA_DOS: DoS defence settings; not converted: syn-flood 300, null-scan
  MOXA_DPI: the Modbus filter (DPI) checks Modbus TCP traffic beyond addresses and ports; not converted: modbus-filter 1
  MOXA_L2_POLICY: the Layer 2 policy filters bridged traffic before the Layer 3 policy; not converted: l2-filter 1 enable

Notes
  (other zones): zones the policy does not name. A rule for 'any' zone applies to them as well.
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
