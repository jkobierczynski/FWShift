From reading the configuration
  HISECOS_SOURCE: 13 rules of the packet filter, 12 of them assigned and enabled, in the order of their priorities; zones are the routing interfaces (port1_1, port1_2, port1_3, vlan20); IPv4 only. The keywords of the commands are FWShift's reading of the MIB and the command list of the CLI reference, not confirmed by its Format lines
  HISECOS_DEFAULT: what no rule matches meets the default policy, drop (packet-filter l3 defaultpolicy)
  HISECOS_NAT_ORDER: destination NAT and 1:1 NAT are read as done before the packet filter, whose rules name the translated address (HiSecOS is built on netfilter; not confirmed): the rules were rewritten for the address as it arrives. Masquerading is done after it. The NAT rules are read in the order 1:1 NAT, double NAT, destination NAT, masquerading, each by priority (an assumption)
  HISECOS_MANAGEMENT: the device's own management access (network management access) is not converted; entries: entry 1
  HISECOS_NOT_CONVERTED: settings that change what passes and are not converted: deep packet inspection (enforcer profiles)
  HISECOS_EDITS: commands that change or delete rules, carried out in the order of the script: delrule 140
  HISECOS_DIRECTION_ORDER: the policy mixes assignments for ingress only with assignments for egress only; the MIB does not say how they are ordered against each other, and they are read as one order by priority: rules assigned for ingress only and rules assigned for egress only

Notes
  (other zones): zones the policy does not name. A rule for 'any' zone applies to them as well.
  1 rule(s) never match and are left out; 'fwshift audit' names them.
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
