From reading the configuration
  HILLSTONE_SOURCE: 4 zones (trust, untrust, dmz, lab); 14 rules of the policy, first match in the order of the configuration (not by id)
  HILLSTONE_NAT_ORDER: DNAT is looked up before the route and the policy, SNAT after the route; the rules were read as naming the destination before DNAT and the zone after it, which is what Hillstone's troubleshooting guide shows (one of its configuration examples names the translated address instead): check the rules for translated traffic on the firewall
  HILLSTONE_ANY: the address Any is read as every IPv4 address and IPv6-any as every IPv6 address
  HILLSTONE_PREDEFINED: predefined services read with the ports FWShift knows for them; check them with show service predefined: HTTP, HTTPS, SSH, PING, DNS
  HILLSTONE_REJECT: action reject: read as a deny that answers the client: lab-to-inside
  HILLSTONE_SCHEDULE: rules with a schedule: schedules are not converted, a target writes these rules disabled: app-clients: office-hours
  HILLSTONE_DEFAULTS: what no rule matches, between zones and inside a zone (default-action): deny

Notes
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
