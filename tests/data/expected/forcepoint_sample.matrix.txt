From reading the configuration
  CONTINUE_RULE: Continue rules decide nothing: they set defaults (logging, inspection, timeouts) for the rules below them that match the same traffic. Their logging was given to those rules: 'lan defaults'
  NONE_CELL: rules with a cell set to NONE, which the engine ignores; converted disabled: 'unfinished'
  AUTOMATIC_RULES: the automatic rules of the engine (at the insert point for them) are for traffic to and from the engine itself, and are not converted
  SOURCE_VPN_NONE: rules that match traffic that comes through no VPN (Source VPN: not via VPN); converted for all traffic, as the model has no VPNs: 'ping out'
  NETWORK_BROADCAST: networks without 'broadcast' in groups: Forcepoint documents the option for rule cells, and in a group they were taken whole: grp-inside
  FORCEPOINT_ZONES: zones are bound to interfaces on the engine, which the input does not hold: the zones of the policy have no interfaces here
  FORCEPOINT_ELEMENT_NAT: element-based NAT (NAT set on an element) and the default NAT of the engine are not in the policy, and not read: they translate after the NAT rules, and an element with static NAT also matches its public address in the access rules
  FORCEPOINT_AUTOMATIC: what the engine does by itself is not converted: antispoofing (from its routing), the automatic rules for its own traffic, and replies of allowed connections, which the model allows with them

Notes
  (other zones): zones the policy does not name. A rule for 'any' zone applies to them as well.
  1 rule(s) never match and are left out; 'fwshift audit' names them.
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
