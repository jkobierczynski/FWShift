From reading the configuration
  IOS_SOURCE: 5 zones (OUTSIDE, INSIDE, DMZ, LAB, MGMT) from the interfaces and the zones of the zone-based firewall; 4 access lists bound to interfaces. A connection passes the inbound list of the interface it arrives on, the outbound list of the one it leaves by and the zone-based firewall. Traffic to and from the router itself is not converted
  IOS_STATELESS: access lists are stateless: on the router a connection also needs its replies let through by the lists of the other direction (established entries, reflexive lists, inspection). The converted policy is stateful and allows the replies of what it allows: where the router's lists did not let the replies through, it allows connections that did not work there
  IOS_ZBFW_NAT: the zone-based firewall was read as seeing the inside addresses of NAT (the destination after translation for connections from outside, the source before it for connections to outside). Cisco does not document this; check its class maps against the converted rules
  IOS_PORT_MAP: match protocol was read as the ports of Port-to-Application Mapping (the system table, and ip port-map); the router inspects the application as well, the converted rules match the ports: mailsub, http, https, dns, ssh
  IOS_SELF_ZONE: zone pairs with the self zone filter traffic to and from the router itself and were not read: ZP-OUT-SELF
  IOS_VRF: interfaces in VRFs: the converted policy has one routing table, and does not know which zones reach each other: GigabitEthernet0 (vrf Mgmt-intf)
  IOS_INTERFACES_LEFT_OUT: interfaces that forward no traffic of their own (loopbacks, interfaces without an address) are not zones: Loopback0
  IOS_SKIPPED_ENTRIES: entries that match no packet that opens a connection were left out (the model allows the replies of what it allows): WAN-IN 30: established: it matches replies only
  IOS_COMPOSED: pairs of zones where an outbound list or the zone-based firewall decides as well: the lists composed into one, for each pair: 25

Notes
  20 rule(s) never match and are left out; 'fwshift audit' names them.
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
