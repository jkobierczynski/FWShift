From reading the configuration
  MERAKI_SOURCE: 3 LAN zones (vlan10-office, vlan20-servers, vlan30-ot-cell) and internet; traffic inside one VLAN passes (it does not pass the MX); the outbound table, first match, ends in its fixed default rule that allows the rest; from the internet only what NAT rules allow passes
  MERAKI_VLAN_IPV6: VLAN objects read with the IPv6 prefixes of the VLAN as well: VLAN(10)
  MERAKI_FQDN: host names in destinations: the MX learns their addresses from the DNS answers it sees; a domain without a subdomain stands for its subdomains too: updates.example.com
  MERAKI_CELLULAR: the rules of cellular failover act only on the cellular uplink, after the outbound table: not converted: cellularFirewallRules: 1 rules
  MERAKI_VPN: the site-to-site VPN rules of the organization decide traffic into the VPN: not converted: vpnFirewallRules: 1 rules
  MERAKI_GROUP_POLICY: group policies with layer 3 rules of their own, for the clients and VLANs they are applied to: not converted, and the rules here may not be what those get: servers-restricted
  MERAKI_SERVICES: services of the MX itself that answer on the WAN (firewalledServices): not converted: ICMP

Notes
  (other zones): zones the policy does not name. A rule for 'any' zone applies to them as well.
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
