From reading the configuration
  ZONE_NETWORKS: the networks behind the zones, from the addresses of their interfaces and the static routes: dmz=172.16.30.0/24; external=0.0.0.0/0,203.0.113.0/28; internal=10.10.0.0/16,192.168.10.0/24,192.168.20.0/24. Targets and --compare that need the networks of the zones take them from here where --zone-network does not give them
  MGUARD_SOURCE: mGuard profile 8.6.1.default, router mode; zones internal, external, dmz; tables read: FW_INCOMING, FW_OUTGOING, FW_INCOMING_WAN_DMZ, FW_INCOMING_DMZ_LAN, FW_OUTGOING_LANDMZ. Each table is first match; what no rule matches is dropped; port forwarding skips the packet filter
  MGUARD_INTERNAL_ROUTED: the internal side has more than one network (LOCAL_ALIASES): the outgoing rules also decide connections from one of them to another, so they are read for internal -> internal as well
  MGUARD_IPV4_ONLY: the packet filter of the mGuard is IPv4: 0.0.0.0/0 is read as every IPv4 address, and the rules match no IPv6
  MGUARD_DEFAULT_LOG: connection attempts no rule covers are logged (LOG_DEFAULT_INCOMING); the model has no place for that
  MGUARD_USER_FIREWALL: the user firewall is on, with 1 templates (service technicians): rules that apply to users who log in; not converted. With it on, the mGuard lets through more than the converted policy
  MGUARD_VPN_FIREWALL: 1 VPN connections have firewall rules of their own (FW_INCOMING, FW_OUTGOING of the connection): not converted, nor the VPN traffic they decide
  MGUARD_MANAGEMENT_ACCESS: access to the mGuard itself is not converted (it protects the device, not forwarded traffic): HTTPS_REMOTE_ACCESS_RULES (1 rows); HTTPS_REMOTE_ENABLE yes; FW_ICMP ping
  MGUARD_HOSTNAME: host names in IP groups, resolved by the DNS settings of the mGuard; read as names resolved for IPv4: scada: historian.example.com
  MGUARD_REJECT: Reject: the mGuard sends the packets back and tells the sender; read as reset-both: FW_INCOMING row 4
  MGUARD_PORTS_IGNORED: ports in rules for a protocol other than tcp and udp, which the mGuard ignores: FW_INCOMING row 7

Notes
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
