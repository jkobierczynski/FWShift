From reading the configuration
  SECUREPOINT_SOURCE: 18 rules of the packet filter in 3 rule groups, first match in the order of the groups; zones from the network objects; what no rule matches is dropped (Default Drop)
  SECUREPOINT_IMPLIED_RULES: the UTM's implicit rules (Firewall > Implied rules: its own services, VPN, the threat intelligence filter) come before the packet filter and are not converted
  SECUREPOINT_LOCAL_RULES: 1 rules for the UTM's own services (to or from objects in zones of its own interfaces) are not converted
  SECUREPOINT_NAT_ORDER: NAT is part of the rules: each rule with NAT became a NAT rule, in the order of the rules. DESTNAT is done before the packet filter and HIDENAT after it (netfilter); a rule's NAT is read as applying whatever its action (assumed)
  SECUREPOINT_IMPLIED: commands that change the implicit rules are not converted: group set implied_group "12" active "1"
  SECUREPOINT_RANGE: network objects with an address range a-b (a form FWShift reads, not documented): printers

Notes
  (other zones): zones the policy does not name. A rule for 'any' zone applies to them as well.
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
