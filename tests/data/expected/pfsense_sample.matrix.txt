From reading the configuration
  FLOATING_LAST_MATCH: 1 floating rule is not 'quick': such a rule decides only where no rule of an interface matched, and of several the last one. In the converted policy it stands behind the rules of the interfaces, the last one first: 'Telnet unless a rule says otherwise'
  RULE_NOT_LOADED: pfSense does not load 1 rule of the configuration, which it cannot resolve; they are not converted: 'Spare network, not in use' (its interface 'opt3' is not there or not enabled)
  BOGONS_NOT_CONVERTED: 'Block bogon networks' is set on WAN: pfSense drops what arrives there from addresses nobody has been given, by a list it downloads. That list is not in the configuration, and nothing is converted for it
  PFSENSE_AUTOMATIC_RULES: pfSense has rules of its own before those of the configuration, which are not converted: it drops packets with a source that belongs to another interface (antispoof), packets from or to 169.254.0.0/16, and TCP and UDP from or to port 0; it passes the ICMPv6 messages IPv6 cannot do without; and it has rules for itself (anti-lockout, DHCP, IPsec). Rules for the firewall itself ('This Firewall', the address of an interface) are converted as rules for those addresses

Notes
  (other zones): zones the policy does not name. A rule for 'any' zone applies to them as well.
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
