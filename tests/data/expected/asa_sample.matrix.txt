From reading the configuration
  UNBOUND_ACL: 1 access lists are not bound with access-group (they serve NAT, VPN or class maps) and are not security rules: branch_vpn
  ZONE_NETWORKS: the networks behind the zones, from the addresses of their interfaces and the static routes: dmz=10.20.0.0/24; guest=10.30.0.0/24; inside=10.10.0.0/16,10.40.0.0/16,2001:db8:10::/64; outside=0.0.0.0/0,203.0.113.0/24. Targets and --compare that need the networks of the zones take them from here where --zone-network does not give them. Behind interfaces that are in no zone of the policy, so behind none of its zones (an allow for the zone of the default route does not reach them): mgmt=192.168.1.0/24
  EGRESS_BY_ROUTE: an ASA access list names no outgoing interface. For permit entries with destination addresses the destination zone was taken from the interface addresses and static routes in the configuration
  ICMP_STATELESS: the ASA does not inspect ICMP ('inspect icmp' is not in a policy map): the reply to a permitted ICMP request got through only where an access list permitted the reply too. Every target lets the replies to a permitted request through by itself
  VPN_BYPASS: the ASA lets traffic that arrives through its VPN tunnels past the interface access lists ('sysopt connection permit-vpn' is on by default). The converted rules do not allow that traffic; add rules for it on the target

Notes
  1 rule(s) never match and are left out; 'fwshift audit' names them.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
