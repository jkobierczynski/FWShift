From reading the configuration
  K8S_IPBLOCK_PODS: shop/allow-web: an ipBlock of a ingress rule holds pod addresses. Whether an ipBlock matches pods depends on the network plugin (the Kubernetes documentation says it is meant for addresses outside the cluster); FWShift reads it as matching every address in it
  K8S_SNAPSHOT: the selectors were worked out for the 9 pods of the export: every rule names the addresses those pods had then. Pods created, deleted or moved since, and new labels, are not in it
  K8S_IGNORED: left out: 1 Pod on the host network, 1 Pod that has ended. Policies do not apply to pods on the host network, nor to pods without an address
  K8S_SERVICES: Services are not converted: kube-proxy translates a Service's address to a pod's before the policy sees the connection, and the rules name pod addresses. Traffic through a NodePort or LoadBalancer (web-public) may also reach the pod with a node's address as its source, which the rules do not name
  K8S_SELF_TRAFFIC: a pod can always reach itself; the converted policy has no rule for it (it never leaves the pod)

Notes
  The policy names no zones, so the matrix has one cell. Give zones as networks with --network-zone NAME=NETWORK[,NETWORK...] to see who reaches whom.
  5 rule(s) never match and are left out; 'fwshift audit' names them.
  A rule the model does not hold exactly (its findings say why) is shown wherever its zones reach, on every service, whatever its action, marked ~: what it really does is not known.
  ~ at most this. Part of the rule could not be read exactly (a host name, a dynamic or unsupported object, a rule the parser reported as approximated), the blocking rules above it could not be followed, or the address a NAT rule translates to cannot be read.
  The matrix shows what the policy can allow for some pair of addresses of two zones, not that every address may. It is read from the configuration: routing, NAT that fails and what listens on a port are not in it.
